# Precedents for savings-to-purchase, layaway, product-linked savings and PAYG marketplaces (input for MoloPay, Cameroon)

Scope: 2010–2026. Labels used: [COMPANY CLAIM] means company marketing or self-reported figures. [INDEPENDENT] means academic, regulator or third-party evidence. [PRESS/ANECDOTE] means press reports of individual cases.
Method note: this was a time-boxed search of about 16 tool calls, so several requested items stayed unverified (see each Gaps section).

## 1. Layaway / lay-by in the US, South Africa, Australia and the UK: mechanics, rules, completion and abandonment

### Takeaway
Big-box layaway in the US shrank. Walmart dropped year-round layaway in 2006, brought it back seasonally from 2011 and ended it in 2021, replacing it with BNPL through Affirm. South Africa offers the clearest legal model for a save-first product: payments stay the consumer's property until delivery, and the cancellation penalty is capped at 1%. LayUp is the main digital lay-by player there. I found no independent figure for layaway completion rates. The available numbers range from "up to 25% cancelled" (an academic model) to an unsourced "60% never completed" claim.

### Cited Findings
**United States**
- Walmart ended year-round layaway in 2006 to cut costs and free space for ship-to-store, keeping it only for fine jewelry. It revived layaway for the 2011 Christmas season. [PRESS] — [Progressive Grocer](https://progressivegrocer.com/wal-mart-phase-out-layaway-while-kmart-vows-keep-it-going); [Consumer Reports 2011](https://www.consumerreports.org/cro/news/2011/09/walmart-holiday-shopping-the-return-of-layaway/index.htm); [ChainStoreGuide 2011](https://www.chainstoreguide.com/offthechain/2011/09/how-walmarts-scattered-merchandising-policies-helped-save-kmart/)
- Fees in 2012: Walmart cut its layaway fee from $15 to $5. Kmart and Sears charged $5 for an 8-week contract and $10 for a 12-week one. In 2012 Kmart and Sears temporarily waived layaway fees under competitive pressure. A $5 cancellation penalty is reported, but the source does not clearly tie it to Walmart. [PRESS] — [USA Today via PressReader, 7 Sep 2012](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/usa-today-us-edition/20120907/282097748890374); [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/business/consumer-advice/kmart-drops-layaway-fees-through-nov/g7LMenAOsa3oNx70S1ITzN); [Yahoo/AP](https://www.yahoo.com/news/kmart-waives-layaway-fee-joining-rivals-160426790--finance.html?ref=gs)
- Walmart reduced the number of Supercenters offering layaway in 2020 and ended the service in 2021, replacing it with Affirm BNPL. [PRESS] — [NWA Democrat-Gazette 2020](https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2020/oct/09/walmart-trimming-layaway-service/); [NWA 2021](https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2021/sep/28/layaway-has-gone-away-at-walmart/); [RetailWire](https://retailwire.com/discussion/will-layaway-programs-go-away-for-good-or-bad/)
- Kmart never dropped layaway. Its spokesperson said it "was in the layaway game before them, and never dropped out." [PRESS] — [Progressive Grocer](https://progressivegrocer.com/wal-mart-phase-out-layaway-while-kmart-vows-keep-it-going)
- The FTC treats layaway only through consumer guidance, not a federal rule: the retailer holds goods until full payment, and consumers should ask about the plan and refund policy before signing. I found no federal layaway-specific rule. [INDEPENDENT/regulator] — [FTC search summary; see FTC legal library](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/102-3127-sears-roebuck-co-kmart-corporation-kmartcom-llc-united-states-america-federal-trade-commission). The FTC actions against Kmart and Sears that I found concern gift cards (dormancy fees) and other issues, not layaway. — [FTC Kmart matter](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/062-3088-kmart-corporation-kmart-services-corporation-kmart-promotions-llc-matter)

**Completion and abandonment rates (weak evidence)**
- An academic operations paper (City, University of London) states that "up to 25% of layaway orders are cancelled." It is a modelling paper, not a measured rate for a named retailer. — [Optimal Inventory Decisions when Offering Layaway](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/20878/1/Layaway.pdf)
- "60% of Walmart layaways never completed" appears only in an unsourced Quora answer. UNVERIFIED, do not cite as fact. — [Quora](https://www.quora.com/Why-don-t-stores-like-Walmart-practice-the-lay-away-purchase-system-throughout-the-year-Wouldn-t-it-be-beneficial-for-the-store-to-keep-hopeful-buyers-coming-in?top_ans=204904965)
- LayUp's own pitch says traditional lay-by suffers "high administrative costs and high non-completion rates". [COMPANY CLAIM] — [TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/layup-advancing-lay-by-payments-in-africa/246273/)

**South Africa: Consumer Protection Act (CPA) 68 of 2008, section 62, and Regulation 34 (2011)**
- s62: each amount paid stays the consumer's property until the goods are delivered (subject to s65). The consumer can cancel and must be refunded, minus a penalty. [INDEPENDENT/law] — [CPA s62, Acts Online](https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/62_lay_bys)
- Regulation 34: the cancellation penalty "shall be reasonable but may not exceed one percent of the full purchase price". No penalty applies if failure to pay is due to the consumer's death or hospitalisation. Cancellation notices may be electronic under the ECT Act. — [Reg 34, Acts Online](https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/r293_34__maximum_amount_of_cancellation_penalty_for_lay_by_s); [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/business/personal-finance/your-consumer-rights-in-a-lay-by-transaction); [Legalwise](https://www.legalwise.co.za/help-yourself/legal-articles/understanding-your-right-lay-goods)
- s62 (from the Act text) also covers the supplier's failure to deliver and what happens when the agreed period runs out. The Act text page should be read for exact wording before quoting. — [CPA s62](https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/62_lay_bys)

**South Africa: LayUp (digital lay-by)**
- LayUp digitises lay-by online and at point of sale (Dashpay/Newland POS terminals). Customers follow their payment progress in a dashboard, and payments recur automatically. It targets unbanked or credit-excluded consumers and cites roughly 24% of SA adults as unbanked. — [TechCentral (partly promoted content)](https://techcentral.co.za/dashpay-layup-partnership-retail-markets/249796/); [Ventureburn 2021](https://ventureburn.com/2021/02/sa-fintech-digitises-lay-by-for-merchants-and-consumers/)
- LayUp claims automated recurring payments raise lay-by completion "by up to 60%" (Nov 2020 press release). It also cites client anecdotes of increases "up to 70%" and a "90%" completion rate on its site, with no methodology given. [COMPANY CLAIM] — [Ventureburn Nov 2020](https://ventureburn.com/2020/11/sa-4ir-startup-layup-creates-unique-payment-solution/); [TechFinancials 2020](https://techfinancials.co.za/2020/11/23/sa-born-4ir-tech-disruptor-revolutionises-traditional-lay-by-system/); [LayUp lay-by page](https://layup.co.za/business/layby); [Araxi interview with CEO Andrew Katzwinkel](https://araxigroup.com/article/founder-and-ceo-of-layup-technologies-andrew-katzwinkel-on-the-topic-of-digital-transformation-of-lay-bys-within-the-market/)
- Merchant pricing: no public rate card; one plugin page mentions a 5% fee per payment plan created (snippet truncated, unverified). Funding: a Caplight profile lists about $560k total raised, including a "growth equity" round in Nov 2021 (garbled page, unverified). — [Third-party review](https://www.ecommercedevelopment.co.za/layup-review-south-africa/); [LayUp Magento blog](https://layup.co.za/business/blog/newsflash-for-magento-website-owners); [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/layup-co)

**Australia / New Zealand**
- Kmart Australia still runs in-store layby: minimum $50 transaction, minimum 10% deposit, $3 non-refundable fee. It also accepts Afterpay. — [Kmart AU layby](https://www.kmart.com.au/layby); [Kmart AU FAQ](https://www.kmart.com.au/faq/payment-and-ordering/)
- Target Australia dropped its six-month layby "following customer feedback and a change in sales trends" and faced Facebook backlash (date not confirmed). It now offers an 8-week layby on purchases of $40 or more. — [SmartCompany](https://coreuat-cdn.smartcompany.com.au/retail/target-cops-facebook-backlash-after-scrapping-six-month-lay-by-policy); [Target AU layby](https://www.target.com.au/help/layby-payments)
- Victoria: on cancellation the supplier must refund all amounts paid, including deposits, except a cancellation fee. — [Consumer Affairs Victoria](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/consumers-and-businesses/products-and-services/business-practices/lay-bys); also [Queensland Govt](https://qld.gov.au/law/your-rights/consumer-rights-complaints-and-scams/buying-products-and-services/understanding-sales-practices/buying-on-lay-by); [SA Govt](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/rights-and-law/consumer-rights/buying/lay-by-sales); [NT Govt](https://nt.gov.au/law/rights/lay-by-agreements)

### Inferences
- The US story suggests that store-held layaway is expensive to run (storage, admin, abandoned items) and loses out once cheap BNPL credit is available. MoloPay's model removes the physical holding cost (the partner keeps stock, priced at a frozen price) but takes on the price-lock risk instead. MoloPay needs a rule for whether the partner must reserve a unit or only guarantee the price.
- South Africa's CPA is a ready-made template for MoloPay's terms of service: funds stay the customer's property until handover, any cancellation fee is capped (1% in SA), the fee is waived for death or hospitalisation, and electronic notices are allowed. Adopting it voluntarily would be a strong trust signal in Cameroon.
- LayUp's pitch shows that automated recurring payments plus visible progress are the core levers for completion. No independent number backs the size of the effect, though.

### Gaps
- No independent, measured layaway completion or abandonment rate (US retailers, LayUp, or anyone else) was found. The CFPB has no layaway-specific publication in these results.
- UK layby: not researched in depth. The only UK item found is the 2024 Laybuy (a BNPL firm, not lay-by) receivership — [MoneySavingExpert](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/news/2024/07/buy-now-pay-later-laybuy-administration); [Citizens Advice](https://citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/changed-your-mind/check-what-to-do-if-you-have-a-laybuy-account).
- LayUp merchant count, user count and audited completion data were not found.

## 2. African comparables: Lipa Mdogo Mdogo, PAYG (M-KOPA, Sun King), susu/tontines, savings apps

### Takeaway
East Africa's "small-small" products (Safaricom's Lipa Mdogo Mdogo, M-KOPA) are credit with device lockout, not save-first. They work at scale (M-KOPA reports 5M+ customers and $1.5bn+ in credit) but bring a high total cost, lockout and repossession complaints, and rising late payment. GOGLA found about 40% of PAYGo solar customers in Kenya and Uganda struggling to pay in 2024. Informal save-first systems (susu) show both demand and the core risk MoloPay must solve: collectors absconding with deposits in a weakly supervised sector.

### Cited Findings
**Safaricom Lipa Mdogo Mdogo (Kenya)**
- Safaricom launched it with Google to move 2G users onto 4G smartphones through daily payments of about KSh 20 over roughly a year. [PRESS/encyclopedic] — [Wikipedia: Safaricom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safaricom)
- A third-party comparison claims a phone priced at KSh 18,300 in cash ended up costing KSh 38,624 under a "Lipa Pole Pole" Safaricom plan, roughly 2.1x. This comes from a blog/retailer source and is unverified. Another guide describes 50% upfront deposits. Informal retailers widely brand their own instalment offers as "lipa mdogo mdogo" (e.g. deposits from KSh 1,500). — [CentWarrior 2026](https://centwarrior.com/lipa-mdogo-mdogo-phones-in-kenya/); [Carlcare](https://www.carlcare.com/ke/tips-detail/which-phones-are-for-lipa-mdogo-mdogo/); [Phones Store Kenya](https://phonesstorekenya.com/lipa-mdogo-mdogo-phones/)

**M-KOPA**
- 5 million customers and $1.5bn of credit across 5 markets (Sep 2024). [COMPANY CLAIM] — [UG Business / company release](https://ugbusiness.com/2024/09/company-releases/leading-fintech-m-kopa-reaches-5-million-customers-unlocking-1-5bn-in-credit-across-5-markets). It passed $1.6bn in loans by Nov 2025. [PRESS] — [Soul 95.7 FM](https://soul957fm.com/2025/11/24/m-kopa-crosses-1-6-billion-in-loans-as-paygo-market-expands/)
- 2024 Impact Report (survey by Dalberg Research): 92% of customers said financing made technology more affordable and 80% reported improved quality of life. The company also claims 7M+ customers served and 3M+ active. [COMPANY CLAIM, commissioned survey] — [M-KOPA Impact](https://www.m-kopa.com/impact)
- Mechanism: a PayGo software lock disables the device when a payment window lapses, and it unlocks permanently only after full payment. — [Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/finance/0409-48413-beyond-financial-inclusion-m-kopa-built-africa-s-first-small-loans-exchange-and-no-one-noticed)
- Audited repayment and recovery data "remain scarce". Most PAYG operators rely on internal repayment metrics not audited to international standards. [INDEPENDENT analysis] — [Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/finance/0409-48413-beyond-financial-inclusion-m-kopa-built-africa-s-first-small-loans-exchange-and-no-one-noticed)
- Complaints: customers allege phones became unusable weeks after completing payments, and one suspected this followed declining a new loan offer. [PRESS/ANECDOTE] — [ABC Africa](https://abcafrica.co.ke/m-kopa-clients-say-phones-became-unusable-weeks-after-completing-payments/)
- Funding: Standard Bank led more than $200M of sustainability-linked debt. Sun King completed a $156M receivables-backed securitisation in Kenya. — [African Exponent](https://www.africanexponent.com/how-pay-as-you-go-solar-financing-in-africa-is-shifting-from-equity-to-debt/); [Energy for Growth Hub](https://energyforgrowth.org/article/off-grid-solars-poverty-premium/)

**PAYGo solar repayment stress (Sun King, d.light, Engie)**
- GOGLA "Keeping the Lights On" (May 2025; surveys in Q4 2024): about 40% of PAYGo solar home system customers in Kenya and Uganda are struggling to pay. The sample was 2,602 customers of d.light, Sun King and Engie who paid less than 66% of dues over 90 days. Struggle rates are about 20% in the first three months, roughly double at 4–6 months, and keep rising. [INDEPENDENT/industry association] — [GOGLA](https://gogla.org/reports/keeping-the-lights-on/)
- Causes from the same GOGLA study:
  - Stable monthly income is reported by only 33% of struggling customers in Kenya and 29% in Uganda.
  - 40% have other debts.
  - More than 50% had climate shocks hit their income.
  - 27% in Kenya and 15% in Uganda have grid access.
  - About 10% pay for more than one solar home system.
  - Technical problems: 49% in Uganda and 28% in Kenya.
  - More than half in Uganda and about a third in Kenya did not know their remaining balance or term.

  The study recommends structured touchpoints in the first months, staff incentives tied to long-term repayment, and standard metrics (PAYGo PERFORM). — [GOGLA](https://gogla.org/reports/keeping-the-lights-on/)
- Many off-grid solar companies have reported rising default since COVID, and PAYGo carries a "poverty premium" (the poorest pay more or are excluded). [INDEPENDENT commentary] — [Energy for Growth Hub](https://energyforgrowth.org/article/off-grid-solars-poverty-premium/)
- CGAP's PAYGo study in Kenya (Dec 2017, slightly before the window) found lockout a substantial barrier for low-income customers who fell behind, and many never caught up and had units repossessed. — [CGAP "Escaping Darkness"](https://www.cgap.org/sites/default/files/researches/documents/Forum-Escaping-Darkness-Dec-2017.pdf)
- A claim that rural PAYG default exceeds 28% (2026) appears only on an unsourced blog. UNVERIFIED. — [Streamline Feed](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/rural-off-grid-solar-payg-microfinance-default-crisis-2026)

**Susu (Ghana) and failure modes of informal save-first systems**
- Repeated cases of susu collectors absconding:
  - GH¢156,455 from 35 savings-group members (Accra).
  - A susu manager jailed for four months over GH¢46,444 from 61 customers.
  - Many other cases.

  [PRESS] — [ModernGhana](https://www.modernghana.com/news/1509821/susu-collector-arraigned-for-allegedly-defrauding.html); [ModernGhana](https://www.modernghana.com/news/1509161/woman-accused-of-absconding-with-ghs156445-susu.html); [ModernGhana](https://www.modernghana.com/news/1051689/susu-manager-jailed-for-stealing-ghs-46444.amp); [GBC 2023](https://www.gbcghanaonline.com/general-news/susu-collector-elopes-with-customers-money/2023/)
- Bank of Ghana officials said some susu companies are not supervised by the BoG but fall under the Credit Union Association. The Institute of Susu Collectors has called for self-regulation. These sources are dated. — [MyJoyOnline](https://www.myjoyonline.com/sunyani-residents-feel-duped/); [ModernGhana](https://www.modernghana.com/news/168098/another-susu-scam.html)

### Inferences
- MoloPay's "no credit, product at 100% paid" model avoids the main criticisms of PAYG: lockout, repossession, a 2x total cost and debt stacking. That is a positioning advantage. The cost is that the customer waits for the product, which weakens demand compared with credit (see section 3).
- The GOGLA data suggest that risk peaks between months 3 and 6 and that customers often do not know their balance. MoloPay's savings plans should show the remaining amount and expected completion date at all times and add touchpoints in the early months.
- The susu failures show why MoloPay must make clear who holds customer funds (a licensed EMI or bank, segregated) so it does not look like a collector or a merchant holding deposits.

### Gaps
- Lipa Later (Kenya BNPL) savings plans, Lipa Pole Pole details, Chpter, PiggyVest "Target Savings", Cowrywise and Watu (motorbike financing) were not covered in this pass. No sourced figures.
- No audited M-KOPA PAR or default rate, and no Sun King collection rate, was found.
- No verified sources on Cameroonian njangi/tontines with purchase goals, or on school-fees savings products, were found in this pass.

## 3. Academic evidence: product-linked savings, layaway vs credit

### Takeaway
RCTs show liquidity constraints, not low valuation, hold back adoption of durables. Credit (time payments) can multiply take-up several times over compared with cash, and one experiment found layaway (save-then-deliver) raised demand about as much as credit. Labelled and committed savings help: Dupas and Robinson found a simple safe box raised health savings 66% and a social-commitment "Health Pot" 128–138%.

### Cited Findings
- **Water filters, Dhaka, Bangladesh (Levine et al., reported 2014):** a filter priced at about $28 (market price), sold with the icddr,b hospital as a trusted partner.
  - Lump-sum offer: 12% demand.
  - Time payments: 47% demand.
  - Layaway (partial payments, delivery after full payment): raised demand "just as much as" time payments, which surprised the authors. No exact % was given.
  - A free trial was not needed to raise demand.

  [INDEPENDENT, author blog] — [Berkeley Haas IBSI blog, D. Levine, 5 May 2014](https://ibsiblog.haas.berkeley.edu/try-it-youll-like-it-testing-models-for-sustainable-business-in-asia-and-africa/)
- **Water filters, Kenya (RAND working paper):** a free trial plus rent-to-own payments via M-PESA doubled purchase to 31% compared with a lump-sum contract. Repayment was low, and many customers paid only when a vendor visited in person. [INDEPENDENT] — [RAND WR-1039](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/working_papers/WR1000/WR1039/RAND_WR1039.pdf)
- **Rainwater tanks, Kenya (MRR Innovation Lab, Nyala Vision SACCO; Kremer et al.):** arms are layaway (the tank is delivered only after the full amount is saved in a target account), an asset-collateralised loan, and a hybrid (save toward the loan deposit), plus random price discounts. Results were not found. [INDEPENDENT, project description] — [UC Davis BASIS/MRR](https://basis.ucdavis.edu/project/better-borrowing-promote-access-water-and-improve-dairy-farming-kenya); [MRR brief](https://basis.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk466/files/2020-06/MRR%20project%20in-brief%20-%20Kremer%20Kenya.pdf)
- **Dupas & Robinson (AER 2013, 103(4):1138–71), rural Kenya ROSCA members:**
  - A simple Safe Box raised health investment 66–75%.
  - The Health Pot (savings earmarked inside a ROSCA, with social commitment) raised it 128–138%.
  - Making savings fully illiquid (Lock Box) cost more than it helped for the average person.
  - Present-biased people (about 16%) benefited only from the Health Pot.
  - 39% were still using their box years later.

  [INDEPENDENT] — [J-PAL evaluation summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/constraints-saving-health-expenditures-kenya); [NBER w17255](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17255/w17255.pdf)
- **Tarozzi et al. (AER 2014, 104(7):1909–41), Orissa, India:** micro-loans for bednets (one-year term, 20% annual interest, lender BISWA) led 52% of households to buy, compared with 10.8% buying when nets were heavily subsidised but cash was required. Evidence on malaria outcomes was mixed. [INDEPENDENT] — [J-PAL paper page](https://povertyactionlab.org/media/file-research-paper/micro-loans-insecticide-treated-bednets-and-malaria-evidence-randomized); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:aecrev:v:104:y:2014:i:7:p:1909-41)
- Caveat on durables: in a carbon-financed filter programme in Kenya, only 19% of households (n=4,041) reported still using the filter 2–3 years later. — [Environmental Health Perspectives / PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5332188/)

### Inferences
- The Dhaka result (layaway about as good as credit at lifting demand from 12%) is the closest evidence that a MoloPay-style save-then-receive offer can unlock demand without lending. It is a single unpublished-detail result from a trusted hospital channel, though. Trust in the seller and in the custody of funds was likely part of the effect.
- Dupas & Robinson suggest earmarking plus social commitment (group or ROSCA-like features, a "pot" visible to peers) works better than pure illiquidity. MoloPay could offer optional group/njangi-style plans or "sponsors" who can top up, while letting savers withdraw in emergencies under a capped fee.
- The RAND result warns that mobile-money instalments without human follow-up see weak payment discipline. Partner and agent touchpoints still matter.

### Gaps
- Devoto, Duflo, Dupas, Parienté & Pons, "Happiness on Tap" (Morocco water connections on credit): not retrieved in this pass. No numbers cited.
- Guiteras, Levine, Polley & Quistorff: the exact paper was not located. The Levine blog is the closest source found.
- No published completion rates for layaway arms in any RCT were found.

## 4. Mechanics, risks and regulation of customer prepayments

### Takeaway
The design levers with evidence or regulatory support are: automated recurring collection, visible progress and balance, a small minimum deposit (Kmart AU uses 10%), a capped and transparent cancellation fee with hardship waivers, and full refund of prepayments. The biggest structural risk is merchant insolvency. In Australia, layby customers of collapsed retailers rank as unsecured creditors. Funds held outside the merchant, in segregated or trust accounts, are the remedy.

### Cited Findings
- **Insolvency:** when a retailer collapses, customers paying off layby are unsecured creditors who must register claims, and in the Allphones collapse layby buyers were not a priority. [INDEPENDENT/consumer group] — [CHOICE](https://choice.com.au/shopping/consumer-rights-and-advice/your-rights/buying-guides/what-to-do-when-a-company-collapses)
- **NZ insolvency rule:** if stock is insufficient, layby customers who started first get priority, as long as they have not missed a payment in the last three months. — [NZ Consumer Protection: gone out of business](https://consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/common-consumer-issues/gone-out-of-business); [NZ laybys](https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/ways-to-buy-and-pay/laybys)
- **Legal ownership of prepayments:** South Africa's CPA s62 keeps each payment the consumer's property until delivery. — [CPA s62](https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/62_lay_bys)
- **Cancellation fee benchmarks:**
  - SA: at most 1% of price, none on death or hospitalisation. — [Reg 34](https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/r293_34__maximum_amount_of_cancellation_penalty_for_lay_by_s)
  - Kmart AU: $3 non-refundable fee and 10% minimum deposit. — [Kmart AU](https://www.kmart.com.au/layby)
  - US (2012): $5–$15 service fees. — [USA Today 2012](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/usa-today-us-edition/20120907/282097748890374)
- **Drivers of missed payments (PAYGo):** unstable income, other debts, climate shocks, product faults, and not knowing the balance or term. The first months are critical. — [GOGLA 2025](https://gogla.org/reports/keeping-the-lights-on/)
- **Collection behaviour:** in Kenya, M-PESA rent-to-own payers often paid only when a vendor visited. — [RAND](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/working_papers/WR1000/WR1039/RAND_WR1039.pdf)
- **Fixed vs flexible schedules:** Target AU removed a 6-month layby (long horizon) and kept 8 weeks. US retailers used 8- and 12-week contracts. — [Target AU](https://www.target.com.au/help/layby-payments); [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/business/consumer-advice/kmart-drops-layaway-fees-through-nov/g7LMenAOsa3oNx70S1ITzN)

### Inferences
- Lessons for MoloPay's UX and marketplace design:
  1. Hold customer funds with a licensed EMI or bank in a segregated or escrow-like account, never with the partner merchant, and say so in the app.
  2. Write into the terms that money stays the customer's property until handover (as in SA s62).
  3. Cap and display any cancellation fee upfront (SA's 1% is a defensible benchmark) and waive it for death or hospitalisation.
  4. Use a small first deposit plus auto-debit or reminder nudges via mobile money.
  5. Show a progress bar, the remaining balance and the projected date.
  6. Use touchpoints in months 1–6.
  7. Set a maximum plan duration or a price-lock expiry, since long open-ended horizons appear to be abandoned. This is an inference from retailers shortening terms.
  8. Define what happens if a partner fails: refund or substitute product, and who bears price-lock risk.
- The one-time handover code addresses the "paid but not delivered" risk and fraud at handover. No direct precedent was found to benchmark it.

### Gaps
- Abandonment reasons specific to layaway (as opposed to PAYGo credit) were not found in any survey.
- I found no quantitative evidence comparing fixed vs flexible schedules or the effect of reminders on layaway completion. Reminder-effect literature (e.g. Karlan et al. on savings reminders) was not retrieved in this pass.
- Regulation of customer prepayments in CEMAC/Cameroon (COBAC/BEAC e-money rules on fund segregation) is out of scope here and not researched.
- No specific collapsed layaway scheme (e.g. Dick Smith or Toys R Us layby outcomes) was documented with figures.
