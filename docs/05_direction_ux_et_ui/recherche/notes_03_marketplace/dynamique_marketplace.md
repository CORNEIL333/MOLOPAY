# Two-sided marketplace dynamics and operations: implications for launching MoloPay's marketplace in Cameroon

Scope: cold start and liquidity, catalogue quality, ranking and merchandising ethics, reviews and trust, take rates and leakage, hyperlocal discovery. Context: MoloPay's marketplace lists offers from verified merchants, landlords, schools and motorbike dealers; customers save toward an offer; launch in Douala and Yaoundé with 4 "universes" at once.
Labels: [EMPIRICAL] = data or study; [FRAMEWORK/OPINION] = practitioner framework or opinion; [REGULATION] = legal text or regulator guidance; [VENDOR CLAIM] = company-reported, observational, self-interested.

---

## 1. Cold start and liquidity: constrain first or launch broad? supply-first? concierge onboarding?

### Takeaway
Practitioner evidence strongly favours constraining the launch (one city or one category) and concentrating on the scarce side, which is usually supply. Launching 4 universes in 2 cities at once goes against what almost every successful marketplace in Lenny Rachitsky's sample did, unless each universe-city pair is treated as its own "atomic network" with its own density target.

### Cited Findings
- [FRAMEWORK] Andrew Chen (a16z), *The Cold Start Problem* (2021): the "atomic network" is the smallest stable, self-sustaining network, with just enough of the right participants that the product delivers its core value and people keep coming back. Large networks are collections of small ones: "Uber is hundreds of city-level markets, Slack is millions of team-sized networks." — [Lenny's Newsletter excerpt, "Atomic network"](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/atomic-network); [TechCrunch interview with Andrew Chen, Dec 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/12/08/andrew-chen-of-a16z-on-how-startups-get-past-a-cold-start-to-survive-and-thrive)
- [EMPIRICAL, secondary, unverified] Slack retention threshold: 93% of teams kept using Slack after exchanging 2,000 messages (Butterfield, cited in Chen's book). Only a single secondary source; check against the book before using. — [CommonWealth Crossing (zh)](https://crossing.cw.com.tw/article/17756)
- [EMPIRICAL, interviews, 2019] Lenny Rachitsky interviewed people from 17 top marketplaces (Airbnb, Uber, Etsy, OpenTable, Rover, Instacart, TaskRabbit, Thumbtack and others). "Every single marketplace" except Thumbtack constrained its launch. Of the 11 cases discussed in the constraint section, 7 constrained by geography (Rover in Seattle, Airbnb in NYC, Uber, OpenTable, Instacart, Breather in the Flatiron District, Zillow with one market) and 3 by category (TaskRabbit, Eventbrite, and Etsy with vintage, craft supplies and handmade). — [Lenny's Newsletter, "How to kickstart and scale a marketplace business"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace)
- [EMPIRICAL, interviews] Liquidity thresholds that were reported: OpenTable needed about 50-100 restaurants concentrated in one city before a consumer found meaningful selection. Uber aimed for more than 30 drivers and an ETA under 15 minutes in each new market. Thumbtack's counter-example: broad categories lifted usage from about once every couple of years to 8-12 times a year, because each single category is used too rarely to build a habit. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace)
- [EMPIRICAL, interviews] About 80% of the marketplaces focused early on the constrained side, usually supply, and built liquidity in a narrow niche before expanding. The median number of levers used to kickstart supply was 2 (average 2.5). About a third piggy-backed on an existing network (mostly Craigslist), about a third used referral programmes, and about a quarter subsidised supply. Rover was demand-constrained: "Supply was easy." — [Lenny's Newsletter, Part 2: Supply vs Demand](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace-9ee); [Andrew Chen guest post, "28 ways to grow supply"](https://andrewchen.com/grow-marketplace-supply/amp)
- [FRAMEWORK/OPINION] Li Jin (a16z): the best consumer marketplaces end up supply-constrained because strong product/market fit creates demand that outruns supply. Later on, many stay supply-constrained (Uber/Lyft, OpenTable, DoorDash), while others see the imbalance vary by geography or category. — [Lenny's Newsletter, Part 2](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace-9ee); [Lenny, "How to know if you're supply or demand constrained"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-know-if-youre-supply-or-demand)
- [EMPIRICAL, anecdote] Manual, unscalable supply work: Zillow hand-entered mortgage quotes every day for its first market. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace)

### Inferences
- MoloPay's 4 universes (merchants, landlords/housing, schools, motorbikes) x 2 cities = 8 separate atomic networks. Liquidity has to be measured per universe-city pair (for example, enough credible motorbike offers in Douala that a saver finds one at their price), not across the whole platform.
- Savings-toward-a-purchase demand comes from MoloPay's existing wallet base and is probably not the scarce side at launch. Verified, well-presented supply is the likely constraint, which points to supply-first concierge onboarding (field agents photograph and list offers on behalf of partners, like Zillow's manual entry).
- The Thumbtack exception matters: a single universe like "schools" is used once a year. Having several universes may help app engagement, which could justify launching 4 at once, but only if each one reaches a minimum density. A defensible compromise is to launch all 4 universes but with narrow scope inside each (for example, motorbikes limited to 2-3 dealers and the best-selling models, and schools limited to a set of neighbourhoods).
- Suggested launch metrics, inspired by OpenTable and Uber: a minimum count of verified offers per universe-city before marketing to demand (to be calibrated; no African benchmark found), the share of searches that return at least N relevant offers, and the share of savings goals that are completed and redeemed.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous (non-interview) causal study comparing narrow and broad launches, and no Africa-specific cold-start case study (Jumia, Glovo, Lipa Later and so on) with numbers.
- I could not fetch the a16z "marketplace 100" or a16z marketplace-guide numbers in this pass.
- No benchmark exists for liquidity in "save-to-buy" or layaway-style marketplaces.

## 2. Catalogue quality: listing quality and conversion, and helping small sellers

### Takeaway
Better photos measurably raise demand. Independent estimates put the effect at roughly 9% (Airbnb, structural model) or a few percent higher sell-through (eBay); platform-reported figures are higher (about 19-21% on Airbnb). The cover photo matters most. Airbnb's experience shows that even free professional photography goes unused unless the platform actively runs it, which supports assisted listing.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR CLAIM, observational] Airbnb says hosts with professional photos earn about 21% more and get about 19% more bookings than comparable listings, comparing more than 14,700 listings that used the service in 2024-2025 with more than 62,300 that did not. Airbnb states that these "are not guaranteed results." An earlier version (sample of about 5,000 listings, Sep 2020-Oct 2021, active 8 weeks before and after) claimed 20% more bookings. — [Airbnb Pro Photo Program](https://www.airbnb.com/professional_photography); [Airbnb Help article 3381](https://www.airbnb.co.in/help/article/3381)
- [EMPIRICAL, dissertation] Shunyuan Zhang (CMU Tepper dissertation, 2019): high-quality professional images raise present demand by about 9% compared with amateur images. Many hosts kept amateur photos even when Airbnb offered professional photography for free. — [CMU dissertation PDF](https://www.cmu.edu/tepper/programs/phd/program/assets/dissertations/2019-business-technologies-zhang-dissertation.pdf)
- [VENDOR ANECDOTE, unverified] Airbnb's early NYC pilot of professional photos reportedly doubled monthly revenue in that market. This comes from a photography vendor's blog, so treat it as folklore. — [Snappr blog](https://www.snappr.com/blog/photography-that-launched-an-empire-how-airbnb-transformed-their-business-with-professional-photography)
- [EMPIRICAL] Li, Simchi-Levi, Wu and Zhu (2023, *Management Science*, Airbnb): the cover photo has a significantly larger effect on demand than any other photo in the listing. — cited via [Let's Enhance research summary](https://letsenhance.io/blog/all/photo-quality-marketplace-statistics/) (secondary; primary not fetched)
- [EMPIRICAL] Ma et al. (Cornell Tech with eBay researchers, arXiv 1811.10648): higher image quality is associated with a higher probability of an item selling, though view count predicts sales better. Category estimates reported secondhand: shoes with higher-quality images were 1.17x more likely to sell, handbags 1.25x. An eBay-attributed figure of 4.5% more likely to sell is also cited secondhand. Observational. — [arXiv 1811.10648](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1811.10648); [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/node/307066); [Futurity](https://www.futurity.org/photo-quality-online-1951282/)
- [EMPIRICAL] Zakrewsky et al. (2016, Etsy): models combining image features (colour, simplicity, composition, aesthetics) with text predicted listing popularity better than text alone. — via [Let's Enhance summary](https://letsenhance.io/blog/all/photo-quality-marketplace-statistics/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- For MoloPay, catalogue quality is mostly a partner-tools problem. Small Cameroonian merchants, landlords and schools will list from smartphones. Useful tools: a guided photo flow (cover-photo framing guide, minimum photo count, blur and darkness detection), required structured attributes per universe (motorbike: brand, model, cc, new or used, warranty; housing: neighbourhood, rooms, water/electricity, deposit or advance-rent months; school: level, annual fees, payment schedule, registration deadline), and a listing completeness score shown to the partner and used in ranking.
- Concierge photography by MoloPay field agents for the first N partners in each universe is the closest analogue to Airbnb's pro-photo programme. Airbnb's experience shows it has to be pushed actively, because offering it is not enough.
- Price and terms must be standardised (total price in FCFA, what the savings unlock, validity date of the offer), because savers commit over weeks or months and a stale price breaks trust.

### Gaps
- No randomised experiment on photo quality was found. All the figures above are observational or model-based.
- No study was found on listing completeness (number of attributes filled) and conversion, and no catalogue data standard for African marketplaces (for example GS1 adoption by Jumia) was retrieved.
- Etsy and eBay seller-education programmes (templates, photo guides) were not documented with outcome numbers.

## 3. Ranking and merchandising ethics: sponsored listings, self-preferencing, ranking transparency

### Takeaway
The EU and US rules give a clear blueprint even though Cameroon is not bound by them. Disclose the main ranking parameters and their relative importance, disclose whether payment can influence ranking, label every ad clearly and individually, explain how recommendations work, and treat the platform's own offers the same way as others or disclose any difference.

### Cited Findings
- [REGULATION] EU Platform-to-Business Regulation 2019/1150, Art. 5(1): online intermediation services must set out in their terms "the main parameters determining ranking and the reasons for the relative importance of those main parameters as opposed to other parameters." Art. 5(3): they must describe any possibility to influence ranking against "direct or indirect remuneration" paid by business users and the effects of such remuneration on ranking. The regulation does not restrict which parameters may be used; the goal is predictability. Changes must be notified to business users in advance. — [McCann FitzGerald summary](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/disputes/new-ranking-guidelines-for-online-platforms-and-search-engines-the-p2b-regulation); [Art. 5 text (gesetze.legal)](https://gesetze.legal/eu/vo_eu_2019_1150/5); [EUR-Lex 2019/1150](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2019/1150/oj/eng)
- [REGULATION] Commission Ranking Guidelines 2020/C 424/01 clarify "ranking," "main parameters," "relative importance" and "adequate understanding," and Annex A gives example parameters. — [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/P2B-Regulation-Commission-Consults-Industry-on-Ranking-Guidelines-11-13-2019); [M. B. Kaya summary](https://mbkaya.com/it-law-eu-commission-p2b-ranking-transparency-guideline-2020-c-424-01/)
- [REGULATION] P2B Regulation, Art. 7 (differentiated treatment): platforms must describe any differentiated treatment they give to their own goods or services, or to businesses they control, compared with other business users. — [Noerr summary](https://www.noerr.com/en/newsroom/news/the-p2b-regulation); [Fieldfisher](https://fieldfisher.com/en/insights/the-p2b-regulation-has-come-into-force-new-obligat) (summaries; exact article text not fetched)
- [REGULATION] EU Digital Services Act: Art. 26 requires platforms to show, for each individual ad, that it is an ad, on whose behalf it is shown, who paid, and the main targeting parameters. Art. 27 requires platforms to set out in their terms the main parameters of their recommender systems and any options users have to change them. Art. 27 applies to all online platforms, not only very large ones. — [arXiv 2503.05764, "The EU DSA: what does it mean for online advertising"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05764); [Freshfields, "DSA decoded #10"](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/dsa-decoded-10-algorithmic-transparency-under-the-dsa-102mgg8); [Pinsent Masons](https://pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/how-the-digital-services-act-changes-things-for-platforms) (notes overlap with P2B Art. 5)
- [REGULATION, US guidance] FTC letters to search engines (June 2013, updating 2002 guidance): ads must be "clearly and prominently" distinguished from natural results, with prominent shading or borders plus text labels. Failing to do so may be deceptive under FTC Act Section 5. — [FTC letter PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/attachments/press-releases/ftc-consumer-protection-staff-updates-agencys-guidance-search-engine-industryon-need-distinguish/130625searchenginespecializedletter.pdf); [Crowell client alert](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/ftc-updates-guidance-to-search-engine-industry-on-the-need-to-distinguish-between-advertisements-and-search-results)

### Inferences
- A defensible MoloPay ranking policy, published in plain French and English for partners and customers:
  1. Hard filters the user controls: universe, city or neighbourhood, price range or "affordable with my current savings plan."
  2. Default ranking: relevance to the query or goal, then proximity or same city, then availability (in stock, places left, offer valid long enough to cover the savings horizon), then listing quality score and partner reliability (fulfilment and redemption rate, complaints).
  3. Price shown as a sort option, never hidden.
  4. Sponsored placements, if introduced, clearly labelled "Sponsorisé," capped per page, and only for offers that pass the same eligibility filters.
- Self-preferencing risk: if MoloPay or affiliates ever list their own offers (for example MoloPay-negotiated "deals"), disclose the differentiated treatment in the spirit of P2B Art. 7.
- Dark patterns to avoid in a savings product: fake scarcity ("only 2 left") unless real, countdowns that push users into savings plans they cannot afford, and default-selecting the most expensive option. Ranking by "affordability given my savings pace" is a fairness feature particular to a savings product.
- Fairness to small merchants: a quality score based on things any merchant can control (photos, attributes, responsiveness) is fairer than one based on volume alone. A "new partner boost" for a limited time helps new supply escape the zero-sales trap.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the EUR-Lex text directly (the page returned empty). Article wording above comes from summaries and secondary sites, so verify it against EUR-Lex.
- No Cameroonian or CEMAC rule on ranking transparency or ad disclosure was researched. Cameroon's law 2010/021 on e-commerce and its consumer-protection framework should be checked by local counsel.
- No empirical study was found on the effect of sponsored-listing intensity on small-merchant welfare in marketplaces.

## 4. Reviews and ratings: cold start, verification, distributions, manipulation, regulation

### Takeaway
Raw review averages are biased (J-shaped), fake reviews work in the short term and are mostly bought by low-quality products, and platforms remove them slowly. EU and US law now treat fake reviews, and unverified "verified" claims, as unlawful. MoloPay has a structural advantage: every review can be tied to a completed savings-and-redemption transaction.

### Cited Findings
- [EMPIRICAL] Hu, Pavlou and Zhang (Amazon data; CACM 2009; MIS Quarterly 2017): almost all products show an asymmetric bimodal (J-shaped) rating distribution, caused by purchasing bias (buyers already like the product) and under-reporting bias (people with extreme views "brag or moan"). The mean is therefore a biased estimate of quality. In a lab where everyone rated a randomly assigned product, ratings were roughly normal. — [CACM, "Overcoming the J-shaped distribution of product reviews"](https://cacmb4.acm.org/magazines/2009/10/42353-overcoming-the-j-shaped-distribution-of-product-reviews); [NYU working paper](https://archive.nyu.edu/bitstream/2451/14951/2/usedbook1.pdf); [MIT Sloan Review commentary](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/the-problem-with-online-ratings-2)
- [EMPIRICAL] He, Hollenbeck and Proserpio, "The Market for Fake Reviews," *Marketing Science* 41(5): 896-921 (2022). Research assistants infiltrated Facebook groups where Amazon sellers bought reviews. Buying fake reviews brought a significant but short-lived rise in average rating and sales rank, which faded after about a month. After sellers stopped, the share of one-star reviews rose significantly, especially for young products, which suggests manipulation is mostly used by low-quality products. Roughly half of the reviews of these products were eventually deleted, but with an average lag of over 100 days. Almost no buyers of fake reviews were well-known brands. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3664992); [INFORMS release](https://www.informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/Study-Examines-the-Impact-of-Fake-Online-Reviews-on-Sales2); [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/fake-reviews)
- [EMPIRICAL, field experiment] Fradkin, Grewal and Holtz, *Marketing Science* 40(6) (2021), Airbnb simultaneous-reveal experiment (reviews hidden until both sides submit): +9.7% host reviews, +1.8% guest reviews, faster and more accurate reviews, and only small negative effects on ratings (about -1.5 percentage points of five-star ratings in the working paper). There was no significant effect on later transactions or adverse selection. Airbnb kept the system. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2939064); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/933743); [NBER working paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f81348/f81348.pdf)
- [REGULATION, US] FTC Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465), announced 14 Aug 2024 and effective 21 Oct 2024. It bans fake or false reviews and testimonials (including AI-generated ones), buying positive or negative reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, review suppression, and fake social-media indicators. Civil penalties apply to knowing violations, up to $51,744 per violation (inflation-adjusted, 2024). — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials); [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews)
- [REGULATION, EU] Omnibus Directive 2019/2161, applicable from 28 May 2022, amended the UCPD. Traders that give access to reviews must say whether and how they ensure reviews come from consumers who actually used or bought the product. Annex I items 23b and 23c blacklist claiming reviews are from real users without reasonable and proportionate checks, and submitting or commissioning fake reviews or misrepresenting reviews (for example, showing only positive ones). Enforcement is uneven: a consumer group reported that the Spanish authority took no action, while the Dutch ACM has acted. — [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/en_US/notes/tmt/2020-06/the-omnibus-directive-a-new-deal-for-eu-consumers-part-2-the-objectives-and-provisions-of-the-omnibus-directive-in-focus); [Bird & Bird on ACM](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/netherlands/acm-takes-action-against-fake-reviews); [Euroconsumers](https://euroconsumers.org/?p=3505)

### Inferences
- MoloPay should allow reviews only after a completed redemption (savings goal reached, product or service collected or delivered, school enrolment confirmed, lease signed). This makes them "verified purchase" by design, and MoloPay can state that clearly, which is exactly what Omnibus requires in the EU.
- Review cold start: new partners have no reviews. Show the "MoloPay verified partner" badge and objective trust signals (verification date, years in business, number of completed redemptions, on-time fulfilment rate) instead of an empty star rating. Prompt every redeemer to review, at a set moment, to reduce under-reporting bias. Consider a simultaneous or blind reveal if partners can also rate customers.
- Display the rating distribution and the review count, not just the average, given the J-shape. Forbid merchants from offering incentives for positive reviews and from filtering negative ones (both banned under the FTC rule and Omnibus).
- Removal speed matters: the fake-review market works because Amazon took over 100 days to delete. Fast moderation of flagged reviews is a design priority.

### Gaps
- No study was found on review behaviour or trust signals in African marketplaces or among mobile-money users.
- No Cameroonian regulation on online reviews was identified.

## 5. Take rates, fees and disintermediation (leakage)

### Takeaway
Gurley argues for modest take rates as a durable moat. African e-commerce commissions run about 5-20% by category (Jumia) and food delivery about 10-30%. Leakage is real but measurable; evidence suggests it rises when commissions are introduced and is smaller than feared when the platform delivers ongoing value (payment protection, guarantees). For MoloPay the savings feature (escrow, commitment, protection) is the main anti-leakage tool.

### Cited Findings
- [FRAMEWORK/OPINION] Bill Gurley, "A Rake Too Far: Optimal Platform Pricing Strategy" (Above the Crowd, 18 Apr 2013): a high rake is friction because it becomes part of the consumer's landed price, and it leaves an opening for lower-priced competitors. "High volume combined with a modest take rate" is the formula for a durable organic marketplace. — [Above the Crowd](https://abovethecrowd.com/2013/04/18/a-rake-too-far-optimal-platformpricing-strategy/); summary via [Fabrice Grinda](https://fabricegrinda.com/it/great-article-on-optimal-take-rates-in-internet-businesses) (full essay not fetched in this pass)
- [EMPIRICAL, company docs] Jumia charges commission as a percentage of the sale price, varying by category, and only on delivered orders, with shipping contributions and storage fees on top. A 2026 Jumia cross-border cost table lists commissions of 5-20% by category, with electronics about 5-8% and clothing/cosmetics about 15-20%. The Uganda vendor hub gives fashion as 20% in a worked example. Rates differ by country. — [Jumia vendor cost summary 2026](https://vendor.jumia.com/?p=1128); [Jumia Ghana fees](https://vendorhub.jumia.com.gh/fees); [Jumia Uganda](https://vendorhub.jumia.ug/?p=9543); [Jumia Senegal fees](https://vendorhub.jumia.sn/fees)
- [EMPIRICAL, press] African food delivery: restaurants typically pay 10-30% commission per order (TechCabal); Techpoint gives 15-30%. Glovo and Chowdeck declined to comment on their rates. Glovo's Africa head says restaurants are its most profitable category and groceries the least. Bolt Food and Jumia Food left Nigeria in 2023. Uber is reportedly taking over Glovo operations in several African markets including Kenya, Nigeria, Uganda and Côte d'Ivoire. — [TechCabal, "Glovo bets on Nigeria"](https://techcabal.com/2025/05/10/glovo-bets-on-nigeria/); [TechCabal, Glovo thinks Africa will skip traditional e-commerce (May 2026)](https://techcabal.com/2026/05/27/glovo-thinks-africa-will-skip-traditional-e-commerce/); [Techpoint, food delivery sector Nigeria](https://techpoint.africa/insight/food-delivery-sector-nigeria/)
- [EMPIRICAL, quasi-experiment] Xie and Zhu, "Platform Leakage" (Lalamove, a logistics marketplace): when driver commissions were phased in, leakage rose by nearly 4 percentage points, doubling the detected disintermediated transactions. — [Kellogg/PKU paper listing](https://english.phbs.pku.edu.cn/info/3882/48882.htm)
- [EMPIRICAL] Astashkina, Bray, Momot and Salikhov, "A Disquieting Lack of Evidence for Disintermediation in a Home-Cleaning Platform": using location data, they estimate that no more than 1 in 83 relationships formed on the app end in disintermediation. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4244111); [Kellogg PDF](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/bray/doc/disintermediation/disintermediation.pdf)
- [EMPIRICAL] Chintagunta, Huang, Miao and Zhang (2023) measure disintermediation with a field experiment using undercover buyers (results not retrieved). Sekar and Siddiq (Airbnb): showing too much information pre-transaction (for example, contact details) raises disintermediation risk. — [Platform Strategy 2023 paper](https://questromworld.bu.edu/platformstrategy/wp-content/uploads/sites/49/2023/06/PlatStrat2023_paper_27.pdf); [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/a-renter-and-property-owner-meet-on-airbnb-will-they-ditch-the-site); [Operations Research, "Platform Disintermediation: Information Effects and Pricing Remedies"](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/references/10.1287/opre.2025.2046)
- [POLICY EXAMPLE] Upwork defines circumvention as meeting on Upwork and then paying outside without paying a conversion fee, and points out that off-platform contracts lose payment protection and dispute resolution. — [Rework summary of platform policies](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketplace-growth/marketplace-disintermediation)

### Inferences
- MoloPay's leakage risk is high in principle: a landlord, school or motorbike dealer can tell a saver "pay me directly in cash or by MoMo." The savings product has to make staying on-platform clearly better for both sides:
  - For customers: locked and protected savings, a price guarantee for the offer, refund if the merchant fails, and possibly bonus or interest or a partner-funded discount for completing via MoloPay.
  - For merchants: guaranteed demand (committed savers), lower collection costs for schools and landlords, visibility, and the cost of losing ratings and verification if they go off-platform.
- Hold back direct contact details until a goal is close to completion or redeemed (Sekar and Siddiq), and avoid high per-transaction commissions on large tickets like motorbikes and rent, where leakage incentives are strongest (Xie and Zhu).
- Pricing options to test: low commission (about 2-5% on big tickets, higher on retail, in line with Jumia's 5-8% for electronics); a merchant subscription (verified listing plus tools) with a low or zero commission; or a fee charged to the customer (a savings service fee) instead of the merchant. For schools and landlords, a SaaS-like collection-tool fee may be more acceptable than a commission. These are hypotheses to test, not evidence.
- Gurley's warning applies doubly in a price-sensitive, cash-heavy market where the off-platform alternative (cash, MoMo transfer) is free.

### Gaps
- No public Jumia Cameroon rate card (Jumia left Cameroon in 2019), and no verified Glovo/Uber Eats/Bolt Food rates for Africa.
- No data was found on merchant price sensitivity to commissions in Francophone Africa, or on subscription vs commission performance in African marketplaces.
- No data on take rates for layaway or "save-to-buy" products.
- The full text of Gurley's essay and the Chintagunta et al. results were not retrieved.

## 6. Local and hyperlocal discovery: distance effects and location display

### Takeaway
Distance strongly shapes local commerce demand. A preprint on Meituan data suggests exponential decay with a scale of about 1.4 km for local delivery, but robust per-km elasticities were not found. For MoloPay, city or neighbourhood, a pickup or showroom location and opening hours should be first-class attributes and filters.

### Cited Findings
- [EMPIRICAL, peer-reviewed] Chen, Fu, Wang, Jia and Kwan (2025, *Annals of the AAG*): distance-decay effects differ by type of online food delivery retailer in Shenzhen and Wuhan. Service areas are equalised by enlarging those of lower-level retailers and shrinking those of mid and upper-level ones. Decay parameters were not accessible. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/raagxx/v115y2025i7p1696-1719.html)
- [EMPIRICAL, preprint, caution] An arXiv preprint (2607.16764) using Meituan data reports an exponential distance decay with a scale of about 1.4 km and a median trip of 1.47 km. It is not peer-reviewed, and the authors say their per-cell decay parameters were not validated. — [arXiv 2607.16764](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16764)
- [FRAMEWORK] Transport geography: e-commerce flattens the classic retail distance-decay curve, and delivery time becomes a key attraction factor. — [The Geography of Transport Systems](https://transportgeography.org/contents/methods/market-area-analysis/retail-distance-decay-curve-conventional/)
- [EMPIRICAL] Zomato data: delivery time increases by 11.01 minutes once distance passes 5 km (time, not demand). — [Atlantis Press, ICEMCI 2025](https://www.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/icemci-25/126018584)
- [EMPIRICAL, interviews] Uber's per-market target of an ETA under 15 minutes and OpenTable's 50-100 restaurants per city show that "local density" is the unit of liquidity. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace)

### Inferences
- In Douala and Yaoundé, where traffic is heavy and addresses are informal, show the neighbourhood or quarter (for example Akwa, Bonamoussadi, Bastos, Mvog-Mbi) plus a landmark-based pickup point, approximate distance or travel time, opening hours and whether delivery is available. Neighbourhood filters matter most for housing and schools (daily commute); motorbike dealers are a destination purchase, so distance tolerance is probably higher.
- Savings goals are long-horizon, so distance affects the redemption step more than discovery. Make sure the pickup point is confirmed before the saver commits, and show "X savers redeemed here" as a trust signal.

### Gaps
- No distance-elasticity evidence from African cities was found, and none for non-food categories (housing, schools, vehicles).
- No study was found on how displaying store hours or pickup points affects conversion.
