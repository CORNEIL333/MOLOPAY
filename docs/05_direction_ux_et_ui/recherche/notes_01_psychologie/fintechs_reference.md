# Reference fintechs for MoloPay: savings goals, gamification, onboarding, transparency and brand

Scope note: about 20 search and fetch calls. Several primary pages blocked fetching (TechCabal 403, Bayes/City 403), so some figures rely on search snippets of those pages. Each item says whether it is a **company claim** or **independent** evidence. Figures run from 2018 to 2026.

## 1. African fintechs: what they do (goal savings, locked savings, fees, users)

### Takeaway
The African savings leaders (PiggyVest, Cowrywise) built around **named goal plans with lock-in and interest**. Wave won its market with **one simple, visible price (1%)** plus free cash-in and cash-out. In Cameroon the MNO wallets (MTN MoMo, Orange Money) already offer basic savings through microfinance partners. The local "pay in installments" e-commerce offers (Glotelho) have trust problems. MoloPay can borrow the lock-and-goal mechanics and the fee clarity. It should avoid unclear installment and blocking practices.

### Cited Findings
**PiggyVest (Nigeria)**
- Origin: the founders of PushCV, which was failing, launched "PiggyBank" in January 2016. In year 1, about 450 users saved N21m. The app went offline from March to April 2016 after security problems with linked cards and OTPs. Regulators objected to the word "Bank", so it rebranded to PiggyVest in 2019 — [CrispNG, "PiggyVest at 10"](https://crispng.com/piggyvest-at-10-how-a-simple-savings-idea-became-a-financial-ecosystem/)
- Users (company figures reported in press): 1M (2019), >4M (2022), >5M (2024), >6M at its 10-year mark. In 2024 it paid out N835bn, and payouts since launch passed N2tn. Users saved about N44,000 per second — [CrispNG](https://crispng.com/piggyvest-at-10-how-a-simple-savings-idea-became-a-financial-ecosystem/). Another secondary source says ">7M by late 2024" (conflicting, unverified) — [rpublc](https://rpublc.com/nigeria/the-piggyvest-way)
- Product architecture uses access as the design variable. **Flex** is liquid, with daily interest; reviewers report the month's interest is lost after more than 4 withdrawals a month. **Safelock** is a fixed lock, and interest is paid upfront. **Target Savings** is goal-based; breaking it early is reported to cost a 1% fee plus the interest. The app also has fixed "free withdrawal days", reminders and progress tracking. Reported rates differ by source (Safelock 13–20% p.a., Target 9–12% p.a.) — [CrispNG](https://crispng.com/piggyvest-at-10-how-a-simple-savings-idea-became-a-financial-ecosystem/); [Nairametrics review 2022](https://nairametrics.com/2022/09/13/review-piggyvest-simplifies-savings-and-investment/); [brands.ng review 2026](https://brands.ng/piggyvest-review-2026-is-it-safe-legit-worth-it-for-nigerians/); [TechCabal rate comparison 2025](https://techcabal.com/?p=174002)

**Cowrywise (Nigeria)**
- More than 800,000 users when it launched stock trading in March 2025. Savings plan types: regular, halal, "duo" (saving with a partner) and "circle" (group saving). The halal product has more than 33,000 users — [AllBusiness.africa wealthtech report 2026](https://allbusiness.africa/insights/african-wealthtech-2026) (secondary; company figures)

**Wave (Senegal / Côte d'Ivoire)**
- Pricing: **free deposits and withdrawals, 1% on transfers**. Before Wave, analysts called Orange Money's rates of 5–10% "abusive". In 2021 Orange cut its transfer fee to 0.8% and made withdrawals free — [NextBillion, Sept 2022](https://nextbillion.net/news/analysis-francophone-africas-first-unicorn-is-disrupting-the-regions-mobile-money-market-but-can-it-last)
- An independent consultant (Amadou Diop, MNS Consulting) said Wave "brought no new technology". He put its spread down to the 1% offer acting as marketing. Wave gives **physical QR cards** to people without smartphones. It uses app-based payments to get around Orange's USSD dominance — [NextBillion 2022](https://nextbillion.net/news/analysis-francophone-africas-first-unicorn-is-disrupting-the-regions-mobile-money-market-but-can-it-last)
- Users (company claims): more than 6M in Senegal, or 75% of adults (2023) — [African Business, Apr 2023](https://african.business/2023/04/technology-information/senegal-is-experiencing-mobile-money-revolution-says-waves-west-africa-director). About 11M active users in Senegal (2025–2026) — [LaunchBase Africa, Mar 2026](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/24/the-267bn-crown-wave-edges-closer-to-dethroning-orange-in-west-africa/). It became Francophone Africa's first unicorn in Sept 2021 — [TechCabal 2021](https://techcabal.com/2021/09/13/wave-is-francophone-africas-first-unicorn/)
- Downside: lower fees cut agent commissions. One Dakar agent's income fell from about 15,000 to about 6,000 FCFA a day — [NextBillion 2022](https://nextbillion.net/news/analysis-francophone-africas-first-unicorn-is-disrupting-the-regions-mobile-money-market-but-can-it-last). One report says more than 20,000 distributors went out of business (unverified, secondary). Orange later scrapped transfer fees but added a 1% cash-out fee — [LaunchBase 2026](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/24/the-267bn-crown-wave-edges-closer-to-dethroning-orange-in-west-africa/)

**Djamo (Côte d'Ivoire / Senegal)**
- More than 1M customers and 10,000 SMEs (April 2025, company claim); raised $17M. Offers automated savings, investment and business banking — [Partech press release 2025](https://partechpartners.com/news/djamo-hits-1m-active-customers-and-raises-17m-of-equity-to-double-down-on-digital-first-banking-services-for-users-across-francophone-africa); [Semafor, Apr 2025](https://www.semafor.com/article/04/07/2025/ivorian-digital-bank-reaches-1m-users). Microfinance licence in CI (2025). Financial inclusion in CI rose from 41% (2017) to 58% (2024), citing the World Bank — [Africa.com/AMA](https://africa.com/ama/djamo-finances-secures-its-microfinance-license-in-cote-divoire). Launched credit in 2025 — [LaunchBase, Jul 2025](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/07/31/djamo-quietly-launches-credit-as-telco-and-banking-giants-storm-its-turf/)

**M-Pesa / M-Shwari / Fuliza (Kenya)**
- M-Shwari Lock Savings: money is locked for a set period of 1 to 6 months — [K24 explainer](https://k24.digital/heartbeat/how-to-grow-your-savings-faster-on-m-shwari/amp)
- Fuliza (overdraft): 7.9M active users in 2024, up 12% from 7.1M. About KSh982bn borrowed in 2024 — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/fuliza/148080/800-000-more-kenyans-turn-to-fuliza-in-2024-as-economic-pressure-bites); [Radio Generation](https://radiogeneration.co.ke/news/12705/fuliza-usage-climbs-by-800000-users-disbursements-reach-sh982-billion-in-2024). In FY2026, disbursements reached KSh1.47tn (+49%) — [AllAfrica, May 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202605110306.html)
- In FY2026, M-Shwari revenue fell 14.1% to KSh1.9bn and active users dropped to about 7M. Credit (Fuliza) is growing while the savings/loan product shrinks — [People Daily, 2026](https://peopledaily.digital/business/fuliza-powers-safaricoms-lending-growth-as-disbursements-hit-ksh1-47t/amp)

**Cameroon: MTN MoMo, Orange Money, Glotelho**
- MoMo Kash (MTN) launched in Cameroon about 5 years after Côte d'Ivoire as "the first mobile micro-loan and micro-savings service". The savings and loans are legally provided by partner microfinance institutions — [Afrique IT News](https://afriqueitnews.com/tech-media/mtn-cameroun-se-lance-dans-les-prets-et-les-epargnes/); [NextBillion](https://nextbillion.net/news/mtn-cameroon-to-soon-offer-mobile-savings-and-loans-services)
- E-money float: at end of Dec 2025, MTN MoMo and Orange Money held 473bn FCFA in banks (MoMo 280bn, OM 193bn), paid 5–6.5% interest. The banks include CCA Bank (58.5bn), Afriland First Bank (53.9bn at 5.5%) and AFG Bank (56.9bn at 6%). Users get none of this interest, which matters for a transparency positioning — [Ecomatin, citing the CNEF 2025 report](https://ecomatin.net/cameroun-orange-money-et-mtn-momo-percoivent-215-milliards-fcfa-dinterets-sur-leurs-fonds-places-dans-les-banques)
- Glotelho (Cameroonian e-commerce, launched 2018) offers **paiement échelonné** (installment payment). Customers complain that phones still show a "blocking imminent" warning after they finished paying in installments — [237online](https://www.237online.com/glotelho-paiement-echelonne-manquements/); [Camer.be 2020](https://www.camer.be/82835/11:1/cameroon-glotelho-cameroun-repositionne-lachat-et-la-vente-en-ligne-au-cameroun.html)

### Inferences
- PiggyVest's main design lever is **friction placed on purpose**: liquid, locked and goal plans, with penalties for breaking a lock and set free-withdrawal days. MoloPay's "save to purchase" model is a Target Savings plan tied to a specific product.
- Wave shows that in Francophone Africa, **a single price people can remember is the marketing**. Disclosing fees for MoloPay plainly, as one number, could be a key point of difference. The Cameroonian MNOs earn interest on float and pay none to users, which is an opening for a transparent offer.
- Glotelho's complaints point to the main local risk: **delivery and device-lock uncertainty in layaway**. MoloPay should show the goods' status, the delivery date and refund terms throughout the process.

### Gaps
- Kuda, Chipper Cash and Moniepoint: not researched (out of budget). No sourced figures here.
- Smobilpay/Maviance and Kiro'o: no sources found. "Paie à ton rythme" as an exact Glotelho phrase was not found. The current terms of Orange Money Cameroun savings were not found.
- Lock Savings user numbers for M-Shwari are not published in the snippets I saw. PiggyVest has not published its own audited user count.

## 2. Global references: Nubank, Monzo, Chime, Revolut, Qapital, Acorns, BNPL

### Takeaway
Global neobanks show the same pattern: **sub-accounts or "pots" for goals, automatic rules, and fee savings as a headline number**. Independent evidence (CFPB on Qapital) shows that **guaranteed or recurring rules beat spending-contingent round-ups**. BNPL is the cautionary tale: stacked loans, late payments and rising losses.

### Cited Findings
- **Nubank**: 107.2M customers at end of 2025 (62% of Brazilian adults). The company claims it saved customers R$134.7bn in fees. It leads "primary bank" incidence per Bain's NPS Prism (Q4 2025). Proactive fixes cut its customer service contact rate by 37% in a year (company claim) — [Nubank newsroom, 12 years](https://international.nubank.com.br/company/nubank-celebrates-12-years-and-is-brazils-favorite-financial-institution/); [Data Nubank](https://international.nubank.com.br/company/data-nubank-nubank-leads-as-brazilians-primary-financial-institution/). In an independent NPS ranking, Mercado Pago (74) overtook Nubank (72, down 3) in 2025 — [Let's Money](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/noticias/mercado-pago-supera-nubank-nps-satisfacao/)
- **Monzo**: customer deposits up 55% to £25.7bn in FY2026. Savings deposits rose 75% to £15.5bn, held by more than 3.5M customers, out of 15.2M customers in total — [Payment Expert, May 2026](https://paymentexpert.com/2026/05/19/monzo-fy2026-results-revenue-profit/). First annual profit, with "record" numbers of Pots created (no count given) — [Kildare Now 2025](https://www.kildarenow.com/news/national-news/1517810/monzo-cheers-first-annual-profit-since-2015-launch.html). Salary Sorter splits income into Pots for bills, spending and so on — [Monzo](https://monzo.com/current-account/personal)
- **Chime**: Round-Ups to the nearest dollar go into savings. "Save When I Get Paid" moves a set % of each paycheck; a review site says it is 10% of direct deposits of $500 or more — [Chime automatic savings](https://www.chime.com/automatic-savings/); [LendEDU](https://lendedu.com/?p=239446). SpotMe was not researched.
- **Revolut Vaults**: round up purchases into Vaults, which can be withdrawn at any time and hold any of 25 currencies. At launch, Vaults paid no interest (UK) — [Retail Banker International](https://www.retailbankerinternational.com/?p=26334); [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/savings-apps-that-round-up-your-change-how-much-can-you-earn-anu4c5P0ZQ6k)
- **Qapital (independent CFPB analysis, Dec 2022)**: 81% of goals use spending-contingent rules such as round-ups, 41% use guaranteed rules such as payday transfers, and 2% use nonfinancial "behavior" rules. **Guaranteed rules are linked to 1.5–3.5x larger increases** in the maximum saved in a year and in the likelihood of reaching $500 or $1,000 — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/consumer-savings-app-strategies-and-savings-outcomes/)
- **Acorns**: customers verified in 2015 whose accounts were still open in March 2024 contributed $270M in round-ups and $543M in scheduled deposits, and held $426M (company data given to Axios). Scheduled deposits were twice the round-ups — [Axios, Apr 2024](https://www.axios.com/2024/04/08/acorns-worth-it-data-report-investing)
- **BNPL criticism**: a CFPB analysis (Jan 2025) found 63% of BNPL users had multiple active loans at once, and one-third borrowed from more than one lender. Klarna's consumer credit losses rose 17% year on year to $136M in Q1 2025, a loss rate of 0.54% of GMV. In a LendingTree survey, 4 in 10 users paid late at least once in the past year — [Fox/LiveNow coverage](https://www.fox5ny.com/news/klarna-bnpl-loans-missed-payments); [BadCredit.org](https://www.badcredit.org/news/klarnas-skyrocketing-losses-expose-weaknesses-in-bnpl/); [ConsumerAffairs on CFPB rules](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/buy-now-pay-later-firms-push-back-against-consumer-protection-rules-050825.html)

### Inferences
- MoloPay's default should be a **guaranteed, recurring contribution** tied to the user's income rhythm (daily, weekly, or a MoMo cash-in moment). Round-ups can be an extra. This is the clearest independent finding I found.
- "Save first, then buy" (layaway) avoids BNPL's debt problems: no loan stacking and no default risk for the user. This can be a stated **brand position against credit**, in the way Nubank positions itself against banks.

### Gaps
- Nubank's onboarding steps and time, and Monzo Plus details, were not verified. Chime SpotMe, Digit/Oportun and Afterpay were not researched.

## 3. Gamification evidence in savings apps

### Takeaway
Rigorous evidence is **thin but positive**. One randomized field study found gamified goal tracking raised savings toward a goal by about 20%. Prize-linked savings has strong evidence from Mexico, but a Kenyan RCT found no significant effect. I found no rigorous evidence on streaks specifically.

### Cited Findings
- Bayes Business School (City, Univ. of London), 2022: 331 UK participants set a one-month saving goal and tracked it in a web app. The group randomly given a gamified version (progress bars, badges, leaderboard) **saved about 20% more relative to their goal**. The authors later noted that lab-style gamification effects may weaken in field settings — [Bayes news release, Jan 2022](https://www.bayes.city.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/2022/january/turning-long-term-savings-goals-into-a-game-can-increase-consumer-financial-well-being); [City Research Online](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/29823/)
- Prize-linked savings, Mexico: 40 of 110 bank branches were randomly assigned a two-month PLS offer. Account openings and use rose, and the effects persisted over 5 years of tracking — [NBER w31529](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31529/w31529.pdf)
- Prize-linked savings, Kenya: an RCT of 300 men found 37.3% saved in a bank vs 27.2% in the control group. The difference was **not significant**, with no effect on alcohol or gambling spending — [PMC6745050](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6745050)
- Boston Fed (2017, background) on gamified savings ("Save to Win" style) — [Boston Fed](https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/communities-and-banking/2017/spring/the-gamification-effect-using-fun-to-build-financial-security.aspx)
- Industry critique: gamification often drives engagement rather than literacy or outcomes — [11:FS](https://www.11fs.com/article/gamification-in-fintech-financial-literacy-or-just-engagement)
- A registered RCT comparing streak, level and combined rewards in a children's app (n=160) has no results yet — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07509489](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07509489)

### Inferences
- A **progress bar toward a named product** is the best-supported mechanic and costs little. Badges and leaderboards are optional; leaderboards carry social-comparison risks in close-knit communities.
- Lottery or prize mechanics may draw people in, but in Cameroon they risk regulatory issues (gaming law) and gambling associations. Treat them as an experiment, not a core feature.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evidence found on streaks, or on gamification in African savings apps such as PiggyVest's challenges. A "30% savings rise" claim seen in blogs has no traceable source.

## 4. Onboarding: steps, KYC tiers, USSD, local languages

### Takeaway
Tiered KYC is the established way to lower onboarding costs for low-income users. **Assisted registration and a transaction at registration** predict activation. Wave shows that QR cards and app-first flows can reach users without smartphones.

### Cited Findings
- CGAP: regulators adopted "tiered KYC" to ease the requirements and cost of onboarding lower-risk, low-income customers — [CGAP blog](https://www.cgap.org/blog/amlcft-balancing-regulation-with-innovation)
- GSMA: in some markets, remote USSD self-registration works (enter basic details, set a PIN, get SMS confirmation). Low-literacy users struggle to self-register, and **assisted registration at a cash-in point raises activation**. Customers who transact at registration were **26% more likely** to become active — [GSMA, Remote Account Opening](https://www.gsma.com/mobilefordevelopment/programme/mobile-money/remote-account-opening-making-it-work-for-mobile-money/)
- Wave: QR cards for people without smartphones; app-based payments to get around USSD — [NextBillion 2022](https://nextbillion.net/news/analysis-francophone-africas-first-unicorn-is-disrupting-the-regions-mobile-money-market-but-can-it-last)

### Inferences
- For MoloPay: a light first tier (phone and MoMo/OM number), then **an immediate first deposit toward a chosen product**, the "first value" moment. Offer assisted onboarding through merchants or agents.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on onboarding time for Nubank or PiggyVest, on local-language interfaces (Pidgin, Fulfulde, Ewondo) in fintech apps, or on COBAC/BEAC tiered-KYC rules for Cameroon.

## 5. Brand: colors, tone and reasoning

### Takeaway
The best-known fintech colors were **contrarian choices made partly by chance** and kept for distinctiveness. Nubank's purple and Monzo's coral both stood apart from banking's blues. Wave's brand was mostly its price.

### Cited Findings
- Nubank: purple and the name were "unorthodox decisions" for a conservative industry (David Vélez, 10-year letter). Skeptics said "You can't have a purple bank!" — [Nubank 10 years](https://nu.com/en/newsroom/company/nubank-10-years-by-david-velez); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2164311). The 2021 refresh filled in the logomark to show "a more mature Nubank". In a later redesign Nu kept and refined the purple because its brand traits "were becoming category attributes" (purple spread across fintech) — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91631436/purple-is-everywhere-in-fintech-nu-bank-redesign)
- Monzo: staff said hot coral was picked for the alpha/beta card without long-term thought, and the logo came after the card — [Monzo Community](https://community.monzo.com/t/the-colour-of-the-debit-card/12359). Monzo says coral "represents our warmth, our empathy and our human quality" and keeps it distinctive — [Monzo blog](https://monzo.com/blog/weve-had-a-little-makeover). The story that it came from a designer's Nike trainers rests on one source — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/is-hot-coral-a-feature-or-a-liability-a-closer-look-at-the-colour-of-your-bank-card-fbd2f987d359). Shade consistency across card suppliers was an ongoing problem — [Monzo Community](https://community.monzo.com/t/colour-consistency-of-monzo-coral/143818)
- Wave: an outside analyst credits its marketing mostly to the 1% offer itself — [NextBillion 2022](https://nextbillion.net/news/analysis-francophone-africas-first-unicorn-is-disrupting-the-regions-mobile-money-market-but-can-it-last)
- PiggyVest: the name was forced by regulators ("Bank" was not allowed) — [CrispNG](https://crispng.com/piggyvest-at-10-how-a-simple-savings-idea-became-a-financial-ecosystem/)

### Inferences
- In Cameroon the MNOs own yellow (MTN) and orange (Orange). MoloPay needs a color that is not a telco color and not "bank blue". Nubank's case shows a distinctive color can become shorthand for the whole category.
- MoloPay should avoid names that imply a bank or regulated savings ("Bank", "Épargne") unless it is licensed, as PiggyVest learned.

### Gaps
- Wave's official brand rationale (its blue, the penguin logo, its no-advertising policy) was not found in a primary source. Founder interviews returned nothing in search.
