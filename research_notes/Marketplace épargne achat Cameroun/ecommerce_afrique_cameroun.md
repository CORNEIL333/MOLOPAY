# E-commerce, marketplaces and consumer buying behaviour in Cameroon and comparable African markets (for the MoloPay marketplace)

Scope note: about 19 searches and fetches, run in November 2026. Figures carry their year and source. Company claims are labelled "(company claim)". Where sources disagree, the notes say so.

## 1. Cameroon digital and e-commerce base data (penetration, online buying, platforms, delivery)

### Takeaway
Cameroon has near-universal mobile connections but only about 42% internet use and about 20% social media use (Oct 2025). Formal e-commerce is thin: Jumia left in 2019 and no large marketplace replaced it. Buying runs mostly through WhatsApp and Facebook sellers, with cash or mobile money paid on delivery. Among the few Findex respondents who bought online, paying cash on delivery is about as common as paying online.

### Cited Findings
- Population 30.1 M (Oct 2025), 60.6% urban, median age 18.0 — [DataReportal Digital 2026 Cameroon](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-cameroon)
- Cellular mobile connections 29.0 M, or 96.4% of population (end 2025, GSMA Intelligence); 87.5% of them are "broadband" (3G/4G/5G). DataReportal warns this share is not a measure of internet use — [DataReportal Digital 2026 Cameroon](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-cameroon)
- Internet users 12.6 M, or 41.9% penetration (Oct 2025), up 317k (+2.6%) in a year. About 17.5 M people (58.1%) are offline — [DataReportal Digital 2026 Cameroon](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-cameroon)
- Earlier editions: 12.4 M internet users in Jan 2025 ([DataReportal Digital 2025 Cameroon](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-cameroon)) and 12.73 M in Jan 2024 ([DataReportal Digital 2024 Cameroon](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2024-cameroon)). The series is not strictly comparable because Kepios revises its method.
- Social media user identities 5.90 M (19.6% of population; 37.3% of adults 18+), with an audience that is 59% male and 41% female (end 2025). Facebook's ad reach is the same 5.90 M, so Facebook is effectively the social network. Instagram reaches only 611k (2.0%). DataReportal gives no WhatsApp or TikTok reach — [DataReportal Digital 2026 Cameroon](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-cameroon)
- Median fixed download speed 11.01 Mbps (2025, Ookla) — [DataReportal Digital 2026 Cameroon](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-cameroon)
- World Bank Global Findex 2025 (survey run in Cameroon in 2024, n=1,000): asked "Do you usually pay online or in cash when the order is delivered?", 137 respondents answered (those who bought online). Raw unweighted counts: pay online 42.3%, cash on delivery 45.3%, both 10.9%. The World Bank warns these are file counts, not population estimates. The base also suggests only about 14% of the sample (137/1000, unweighted) bought online — [World Bank Microdata, Findex 2025 Cameroon, fin27](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/7880/variable/F1/V95?name=fin27)
- Findex 2025, aggregate figures: digital merchant payments rose from 35% of adults (2021) to 42% (2024) in low- and middle-income economies (LMICs), with "strong growth in economies such as Cameroon". In LMICs, 36% of adults buy goods online, and about two-thirds of online shoppers pay online while the rest pay on delivery — [Global Findex 2025 figure list (World Bank)](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/be6615202d1f08a25855c8ac2d615122-0050012025/related/Global-Findex-2025-figure-list.pdf); [Cyprus Mail summary of Findex 2025](https://cyprus-mail.com/2025/08/18/smartphone-growth-fuels-global-expansion-of-digital-finance)
- An ECA/GSMA/ECCAS report on Central Africa (2021) found social platforms far more used for buying than e-commerce sites. WhatsApp is "the most used channel for transactions in Cameroon". A survey in Cameroon found **88% of respondents had bought via WhatsApp and 68% via Facebook** (survey name, sample and date not given in the article). Merchants sell through sales groups, statuses and stories — [Cameroon Tribune, 27 Aug 2021](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/41994/fr.html/details_2)
- UNCTAD's B2C E-commerce Index 2018 ranked Cameroon 10th in Africa, with its best score on delivery (postal) and weaknesses in online payment and street addressing. Online shops increasingly offered cash on delivery because of a "strong cash habit" — [Osiris / CIO Mag, 25 Jan 2019](https://www.osiris.sn/Cameroun-dans-le-top-10-africain.html)
- Glotelho (local online retailer): its app offers door delivery, the option to "check and pay on delivery", and a 7-day return policy (company claim) — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6444913457). A 2020 press piece says Glotelho accepted cash on delivery plus Orange Money and MTN MoMo — [Camer.be](https://www.camer.be/82835/11:1/cameroon-glotelho-cameroun-repositionne-lachat-et-la-vente-en-ligne-au-cameroun.html)
- A 2025 seller guide says WhatsApp Business is the "de facto CRM" of Cameroonian e-merchants selling via TikTok and Facebook. It advises asking new customers for a 50–100% deposit before delivery to filter out "ghost" buyers. This is commercial advice, not survey data — [Lefisk.cm](https://lefisk.cm/blog/e-commerce-cameroun-vendre-tiktok-facebook-chine-dropshipping)
- A Ngaoundéré University thesis (2020) found that after "the two e-commerce giants" left in November 2019, online buying in Cameroon lost visibility and stalled. It names perceived risk and buying culture as the main brakes, with financial risk as the main fear — [Memoireonline, "Facteurs explicatifs de l'achat en ligne au Cameroun"](https://www.memoireonline.com/07/21/11970/m_Facteurs-explicatifs-de-l-achat-en-ligne-au-Cameroun25.html)

### Inferences
- About 42% of the population is online and about 20% uses social media. A savings-and-buy marketplace in Douala and Yaoundé therefore reaches an urban, young, mostly male connected population first. Offline or assisted onboarding (agents, partner shops) will matter for the rest.
- In Cameroon, buyers split roughly evenly between paying before and paying on delivery. MoloPay's model is "save first, collect in shop with a one-time code (OTP)". It flips cash on delivery around: the customer pays ahead, but the frozen price and the in-shop handover replace delivery-time inspection as the trust mechanism.

### Gaps
- No reliable Cameroon e-commerce market size (in XAF or USD) was found. Figures from Statista-type aggregators were not checked.
- Findex 2025 country tables (weighted %) for Cameroon "made a digital merchant payment" and "bought online" were not retrieved. Only the unweighted microdata counts above are available. Look them up in the Findex 2025 databank before citing.
- ITU 2024/2025 figures for Cameroon (individuals using the internet, smartphone ownership) were not retrieved. No smartphone penetration figure was found. GSMA Mobile Economy Sub-Saharan Africa would be the source.
- Afrimarket: possibly the second "giant" that left Cameroon in Nov 2019 (the Ngaoundéré thesis does not name it). Its liquidation and Cameroon role were not verified in this session.
- "Kiro'o" is Kiro'o Games, a Cameroonian video-game studio, not an e-commerce player (background knowledge, not sourced here). No other large Cameroonian marketplace was confirmed.
- No data was found on Facebook Marketplace use in Cameroon, pickup points or relay-point networks, or delivery-fee levels in Douala and Yaoundé.

## 2. Why Jumia left Cameroon (2019) and other markets; Jumia operating lessons

### Takeaway
Jumia shut Cameroon in Nov 2019, saying its model "is not adapted to the current context". The background was low internet penetration, a slowing economy, the Anglophone crisis, and a pivot from growth to profitability. Jumia later left Tanzania and Rwanda (2019), South Africa and Tunisia (2024) and Algeria (early 2026), each time citing weak economics. Its operating playbook relies on prepaid wallets, pay-on-delivery-via-JumiaPay, upcountry pickup stations, and JForce agents.

### Cited Findings
- CEO Sacha Poignonnec on the Nov 2019 shutdown (company statement): "our transactional platform, as currently run, is not adapted to the current context in Cameroon" — [Africanews, 18 Nov 2019](https://www.africanews.com/2019/11/18/e-commerce-giant-jumia-abruptly-shuts-down-cameroon-operations); [Quartz](https://qz.com/africa/1751659/jumia-shuts-down-in-cameroon-as-african-e-commerce-struggles)
- Reported context: an internal source said Jumia was prioritising profitability over growth ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/ecommerce/2019/amazon-of-africa-jumia-shuts-down-cameroon-ecommerce-ops)). Other reports cite political tensions (the Anglophone crisis) and slow growth ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2019/11/19/like-gabon-congo-jumia-shuts-operations-in-cameroon/)) and internet penetration of only about 24% at the time ([TechCabal FR](https://techcabal.com/fr/?p=64428)). Cameroonian tech figure Rebecca Enonchong criticised the company's lack of local adaptability ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2019/11/19/jumia-exits-cameroon/)). Jumia Deals (classifieds) reportedly continued for a while after the shutdown ([Sagaci Research](https://sagaciresearch.com/internet-retailer-jumia-pulls-the-plug-on-cameroon/))
- Jumia left Tanzania and then Rwanda in Dec 2019 — [Financial Afrik](https://www.financialafrik.com/2019/12/11/jumia-annonce-son-retrait-du-rwanda/)
- South Africa (Zando) and Tunisia were closed by the end of 2024. They accounted for only 3.5% and 2.7% of 2023 orders (4.5% and 3.0% of GMV, the gross value of goods sold). Jumia cited macro conditions and competition, including Temu and Shein in South Africa — [Jumia Group news](https://group.jumia.com/news/african-online-retailer-jumia-to-exit-south-africa-and-tunisia); [Nairametrics, Oct 2024](https://nairametrics.com/2024/10/16/jumia-exits-south-africa-and-tunisia-to-focus-on-nigeria-other-markets)
- Algeria was closed in Q1 2026 (reported Feb 2026), citing a stagnant market — [North Africa Post](https://northafricapost.com/94827-jumia-to-exit-algeria-citing-stagnant-market.html); [Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/news/jumia-ends-algeria-operations/)
- **Prepayment share (JumiaPay used to pay for orders):** 29% of orders in Q1 2023, down from 34% in Q1 2022. In Kenya, 20% of post-paid orders were settled via JumiaPay on delivery in March 2023 — [Jumia Q1 2023 results](https://www.newswire.com/news/jumia-reports-first-quarter-2023-results). The share was 44.7% in Q3 2023 ([Jumia Q3 2023 Exhibit 99.1](https://s205.q4cdn.com/370993272/files/doc_financials/2023/q3/Jumia-EX-99-1-Q3-2023.pdf)) and 45% in Q4 2023, against 31% in Q4 2022 ([TechCrunch, Feb 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/15/jumia-reduces-losses-by-over-90-amid-focus-on-restoring-order-and-gmv-growth)). The rise comes mainly from "JumiaPay on delivery": customers pay by mobile money or card at the doorstep, which still counts as digital. By implication, about 55% or more of orders were still paid in cash on delivery as of late 2023 (inference)
- JumiaPay on delivery was piloted in Kenya and Nigeria, then rolled out to Morocco, Ghana and Uganda in 2023 — [Jumia Q2 2023 results](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/computers-technology-and-internet/jumia-reports-second-quarter-2023-results-774056)
- Upcountry (outside main cities) orders were 54% of FY2024 orders, up from 48% in 2023. Jumia is expanding pickup stations outside urban centres (company claim). JumiaPay transactions in 2024 were 10.1 M (+20% YoY) and TPV, the total value paid through JumiaPay, was USD 195.4 M — [Jumia Q4 2024 earnings call transcript (Insider Monkey)](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/jumia-technologies-ag-nysejmia-q4-2024-earnings-call-transcript-1459763/); [Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/2025/02/20/jumia-2024-revenue-declines/)
- JForce: commission-based sales consultants place orders on behalf of family, friends and neighbours. The scheme is free to join. Tiers run Probation, Bronze, Silver, Gold. Nigerian example: ₦1,000–3,000 commission per ₦100,000 of monthly sales in Lagos, Abuja and Ibadan, and ₦2,000–6,000 in other cities (company claim) — [Jumia Nigeria JForce page](https://www.jumia.com.ng/jforce/). In Kenya in 2018, commissions were 3–10% and more than 6,000 agents had been trained since 2013 — [TechTrends KE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2018/01/28/jumia-j-force-program-to-create-jobs/). Agent counts are inconsistent across sources (40,000 vs 80,000). A 2019 internal fraud case involved JForce — [Brandspur](https://brandspurng.com/2019/08/19/jumia-in-trouble-over-fraud-in-affiliate-scheme-jforce/)

### Inferences
- Jumia exits small markets where it lacks order density. A savings-led marketplace with partner shops doing the handover avoids last-mile delivery costs entirely, the cost that killed Jumia's economics in Cameroon.
- Jumia raised digital payment mainly by digitising payment at the moment of handover (pay on delivery via wallet), not by forcing prepayment. That suggests MoloPay's handover moment (OTP in the shop) is also where trust is earned.

### Gaps
- No full-year cash-on-delivery percentage from the Jumia 20-F was retrieved. Pickup-station counts and return rates were not retrieved either.
- No Cameroon-specific Jumia figures (orders, GMV, cash-on-delivery share before 2019) were found.

## 3. Social commerce: WhatsApp, Facebook, TikTok

### Takeaway
In Cameroon and comparable markets, discovery and selling happen on WhatsApp (status updates, groups, Business catalogues) and Facebook (groups, pages). Women micro-sellers rely heavily on WhatsApp. Social commerce runs on personal trust but has no buyer protection.

### Cited Findings
- Cameroon: 88% of surveyed respondents had bought through WhatsApp and 68% through Facebook. Sellers use groups, statuses and stories (ECA/GSMA/ECCAS 2021; underlying survey undated) — [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/41994/fr.html/details_2)
- Caribou Digital (2026) estimates nearly 90 M women in India, Kenya, Nigeria and Pakistan earn income through WhatsApp, about 1 in 6 working-age women. About 60% of self-employed women in Nigeria use WhatsApp for work — [Semafor, 15 May 2026](https://www.semafor.com/article/05/15/2026/nearly-60-of-self-employed-women-in-nigeria-use-whatsapp-for-work)
- Kenya: 92% of SMEs reportedly use social commerce (Caribou Data / PFDA study, as reported; primary source not opened) — [search result summary, primary not verified]. Treat as unverified.
- IFC "Women and E-commerce in Africa" (2021, with Jumia data) found women sellers on platforms run smaller firms with lower sales. Only about 7% of women had used e-commerce-platform financing. IFC recommends moving women from informal WhatsApp selling onto platforms — [IFC Digital2Equal report](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/mgrt/202105-digital2equal-women-and-e-commerce-africa.pdf); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/women-and-e-commerce-in-africa-the-15-billion-opportunity/)
- MicroSave (2025) report on social commerce initiatives (not opened in detail) — [MicroSave](https://www.microsave.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Final_SCIReport_English.pdf)
- MarketForce (Kenya) shut its B2B arm RejaReja in 2024 and pivoted to social commerce — [TechCabal, Apr 2024](https://techcabal.com/2024/04/17/marketforce-shuts-down-rejareja/)

### Inferences
- MoloPay offers will likely need to be shareable as WhatsApp status-ready cards or links, since that is where discovery happens. Partner shops' existing WhatsApp audiences are an acquisition channel.

### Gaps
- No Meta, GSMA or McKinsey figures on TikTok commerce in Cameroon were found. No quantitative data on discovery through WhatsApp status in Cameroon was found.

## 4. Trust barriers and how consumers verify

### Takeaway
Cameroonians link online buying with scams: sellers who vanish after taking a deposit, fake profiles, doctored photos, counterfeits. Trust comes from merchant reputation, a known physical seller, seeing the product, and paying on delivery. Nobody publishes national statistics on online fraud.

### Cited Findings
- A Yaoundé I thesis (2020, 8 qualitative interviews) found transaction security, merchant reputation and competence, and internet familiarity drive trust. Fraud, non-delivery and card theft are the main fears — [DICAMES, ENSET Ebolowa thesis](https://dicames.online/jspui/handle/20.500.12177/7353)
- Cameroon Tribune describes buyers disappointed by counterfeit products that differ from what was shown on Facebook. Scammers use fake profiles, doctored photos and fake customer reviews to build trust — [Cameroon Tribune](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79827/fr.html/details_2)
- Le360 (Dec 2023, Yaoundé): some residents equate e-commerce with fraud. One seller describes the pattern: "As soon as you order, they ask for an advance. Once that's done, the seller disappears." Opinion leaders call for identifiable e-merchants approved by the Ministry of Commerce — [Le360 Afrique](https://afrique.le360.ma/economie/cameroun-commerce-en-ligne-et-arnaque-font-bon-menage_LAVEL6WQJ5HFBI2EGGPMUMAB3A/)
- The deposit problem runs both ways: sellers demand 50–100% upfront to avoid ghost buyers ([Lefisk.cm](https://lefisk.cm/blog/e-commerce-cameroun-vendre-tiktok-facebook-chine-dropshipping)), and buyers fear exactly that advance (Le360 above)
- Nigeria: distrust targets sellers' delivery promises more than payment platforms. "I trust the online payment platforms…, I don't trust Konga/Jumia to deliver as promised" (2015/16 forum quote) — [Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/2016/02/02/pay-on-delivery-nigeria/). Oxford Business Group, as cited by The Guardian Nigeria (date unclear), reports 70% of Nigerians prefer cash on delivery — [Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/?p=2374523) (figure unverified, date unknown)

### Inferences
- MoloPay's design addresses the core Cameroonian fear (a deposit paid to a seller who disappears) only if verified partners, money held safely in escrow or ring-fenced (not passed to the merchant before handover), and refund rules are visible. The deposit mirrors the scam pattern, so the guarantee must be explicit.
- In-shop handover with an OTP matches the strongest verification habit: seeing and testing the product physically, especially phones, where fakes and refurbished units are a known risk.

### Gaps
- No quantitative Cameroonian survey was found on the share of people scammed online or on counterfeit phones. ANTIC, MINCOMMERCE or consumer-league (Ligue camerounaise des consommateurs) data were not found.

## 5. Price sensitivity and haggling

### Takeaway
No sourced data was found on haggling at Mboppi (Douala) or Mokolo (Yaoundé), or on online price comparison in Cameroon.

### Cited Findings
- None found in this session.

### Inferences
- From background knowledge, unsourced: open-market negotiation is the norm in Cameroon. A fixed online price competes with an expected "discount" in the shop. A frozen price may be valued as protection against inflation and against the price "drift" during negotiation, but this needs field testing.

### Gaps
- Academic or press sources on bargaining culture in Cameroonian markets, and any studies of fixed vs negotiated pricing online in Africa, were not found.

## 6. Assisted commerce models: Copia, JForce, MarketForce, Wasoko

### Takeaway
Agent-led commerce reaches low-income and rural buyers who distrust online-only retail. But Copia shows that running your own warehouses, vehicles and tens of thousands of agents on orders of about USD 10 does not break even. Copia went into administration in May 2024 and was ordered into liquidation in Sept 2026. B2B players (MarketForce, Wasoko) also cut back or merged in 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Copia's average order was about USD 10 in 2019, against about USD 66 at Jumia — [Quartz, 2019](https://qz.com/africa/1757623/kenyas-copia-global-raises-26-million-in-series-b-funding)
- Copia raised a USD 50 M Series C in Jan 2022 (lead investor Goodwell, with Lightrock, DEG and the US DFC). Total funding is reported as USD 83.5 M ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2022-01-21-copia-secures-50-million-for-africa-expansion)), USD 103 M ([AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/copia-globals-50m-round-proves-investor-interest-in-africas-oft-ignored-rural-consumer)) or USD 107 M ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2023/11/15/copia-wants-more-online-orders/)). The sources conflict.
- Agents: 30,000 in Kenya and Uganda, 77% of them women (2022) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/18/kenyan-e-commerce-company-copia-global-raises-50m-to-ramp-up-african-expansion). Over 50,000 agents and more than 2 M consumers served (company claim, Dec 2023) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/12/13/ex-metaswitch-ceo-john-lazar-joins-copias-board-as-the-kenyan-e-commerce-outfit-rakes-in-20m-to-push-toward-profitability)
- Copia laid off 350 staff and cut operations in July 2023, closed Uganda, and dropped plans for Nigeria, Ghana, South Africa and Mozambique. In late 2023 it pushed customers toward app orders ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2023/11/15/copia-wants-more-online-orders/)). It entered administration under KPMG in May 2024 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2024/05/24/copia-enters-into-administration-after-failing-to-secure-funding/)) and suspended service in six regions in June 2024 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2024/06/04/copia-stops-orders-in-six-regions-in-kenya/)). It moved toward liquidation in July 2024 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2024/07/03/copia-begins-liquidation-after-failing-to-raise-money/))
- The High Court ordered Copia Kenya into liquidation on 17 Sept (2026). Weetracker's analysis: about USD 123 M was lost; the company never made a profit in 12 years; AOV stayed near USD 10 while it carried warehouses, depots, vehicles and agent commissions; its peak was 1,800 staff and over 50,000 agents. Founders then launched Stahili, a leaner model with no owned last-mile delivery — [Weetracker, 30 Sept 2026](https://weetracker.com/2026/09/30/copia-kenya-liquidation-123m-rural-ecommerce-math-problem/)
- MarketForce closed RejaReja (B2B for informal retailers) in Apr 2024 after withdrawing from all markets except Uganda, and pivoted to social commerce — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/19/marketforce-winds-down-its-b2b-e-commerce-arm/)
- Wasoko left Zanzibar and paused Uganda and Zambia (Mar 2024), and was marked down to about USD 260 M after an investor halved its stake value ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/27/african-b2b-e-commerce-giant-wasoko-marked-down-to-260m-after-vc-halves-stake)). It completed an all-stock merger with MaxAB in Aug 2024 and pivoted toward fintech ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/27/wasoko-maxab-complete-merger))

### Inferences
- The lesson for MoloPay: keep the agent and assisted-sales layer commission-only and avoid owning logistics. Use partner shops' existing premises and stock as the pickup network. That is the leaner path Copia's founders themselves took with Stahili.
- Savings toward a product raises the effective basket above Copia's USD 10 AOV (phones, fridges, motorbikes), which improves the economics per transaction.

### Gaps
- No Jumia agent-network figures for francophone markets (Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal) were found. No Cameroonian agent-commerce players were identified.

## 7. Most demanded product categories among modest-income urban Cameroonians

### Takeaway
Phones are the clearest category. In Cameroon's usage share, Transsion (Tecno, Infinix, itel) and Samsung lead. No sourced market data was found for motorbikes, fridges, gas or school supplies.

### Cited Findings
- StatCounter, Cameroon mobile vendor usage share (Sept 2026): Tecno 16.1%, Samsung 14.5%, Apple 11.9%, Xiaomi 7.6%, Infinix 6.0%, with 30% "unknown". StatCounter measures web traffic, not sales, and is biased toward heavier internet users — [StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/vendor-market-share/mobile./cameroon)
- Canalys, H1 2025: Transsion shipped 18.7 M smartphones to Africa vs 7.4 M for Samsung, about 51% of the African market — [Ecomatin](https://ecomatin.net/smartphones-le-chinois-transsion-tecno-itel-infinix-controle-51-du-marche-africain-a-fin-juin)

### Inferences
- Phones (Tecno and Infinix mid-range) are the obvious first category for saving toward a goal. Counterfeit and quality fears make an in-shop handover from verified partners a strong selling point here.

### Gaps
- No data was found on Cameroonian household purchase priorities: motorbikes ("benskins"), fridges, freezers, LPG gas bottles, the school back-to-school season ("rentrée scolaire") or tontine-financed purchases. Possible sources: INS Cameroun ECAM household surveys and Business in Cameroon import statistics. These were not reached in this session.
